Feedback after every take, never during. Record every take and give feedback on every one — right after the take, and only after the learner has first marked where they think they went wrong; the "withhold feedback so they don't depend on it" rule hasn't held up (no reliable benefit of reduced feedback frequency), though this does not prove that more feedback is better. Feedback covers only dimensions machines measure reliably (timing, pitch, chord-change accuracy), gives one priority for the next take, and suppresses low-confidence detections; tone and phrasing are prompts for self-review, not scores. No live on-screen feedback while playing. (Context: an adaptive tutor for plateaued adult intermediate guitarists who practise about 2 hours a day for 2 years; feedback comes from AI analysis of recordings.)
