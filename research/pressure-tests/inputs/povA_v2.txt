Hear it before you drill it. For a plateaued intermediate guitarist, nothing gets high-volume drilling until they can hear it and play it back by ear; scales and theory stay in the curriculum, but the ear decides when they get drilled. The bet is that the plateau is more often a hearing problem than a technique or theory problem, so the tutor first screens each learner (play back a short melody by ear, play back a rhythm, a technique check) and leads with the ear only where it is the weakest link. Before the gate opens, the learner may label the concept, compare it with near neighbours, and play it slowly while singing or humming (call-and-response); the gate (e.g. 4 of 5 short examples played back correctly on the guitar; singing optional) controls only tempo ladders, high-volume repetition and use in improvisation. This opposes the common theory-first, scales-first and "more technique hours" advice. (Context: an adaptive tutor for plateaued adult intermediate guitarists who practise about 2 hours a day for 2 years.)
