# POV candidates

Candidate points of view for the BrainLift. They are not final POVs and have not been pressure-tested.

## Contested points from the evidence pass (added 2026-10-07 CT)

Each item names the conventional advice it challenges and the evidence on each side (IDs in `research/evidence.csv`). **These are candidates, not POVs.** They have not been pressure-tested or written up yet.

1. **"Give feedback sparingly — frequent feedback creates dependency" (the guidance hypothesis).**
   - **For:** Winstein & Schmidt 1990 (E023); Wulf & Mornell's music advice (E030); violin real-time visual feedback hurt sound while it was on (E032).
   - **Against:**
     - Meta-analysis k=75: no effect of reduced frequency at any time point; the literature is underpowered (E026).
     - Complex skills benefited from 100% feedback (E024), and simple-task principles don't generalise (E025).
     - In the violin study, the retention benefit came within the *same* session (E032).
   - **Candidate:** for complex musical skills, the dose of feedback matters less than its *modality and timing*. Post-take, audio-anchored feedback avoids the attention-split problem (E033) without needing to be withheld.
   - Relevance: adjacent/extrapolated.

2. **"Let learners control their practice and feedback; autonomy boosts learning."**
   - **For:** Chiviacowsky & Wulf 2002 (E027); E030.
   - **Against:** bias-corrected g = .107, not distinguishable from zero (E028).
   - **Nuance:** autonomy may still matter for *persistence* (E041, extrapolated) and is welcome in advanced adults (E040).
   - **Candidate:** autonomy is a retention/adherence lever, not a learning-efficiency lever. Design for it on motivational grounds only.

3. **"Use an external focus of attention" (imported from sport into music teaching).**
   - **For:** E030.
   - **Against:** no consistent superiority across music experiments (E031).
   - **Candidate:** cue wording in a guitar tutor should be judged on clarity and task fit, not on external vs internal focus.

4. **"Deliberate practice is what separates experts — just practise better/more."**
   - **For:** E042 (r = .61), E045.
   - **Against:** 21% (E043) and 29.9% (E044) of variance; twin evidence (E047, E048).
   - **Candidate:** for a plateaued adult already practising 2 h/day, more hours is not the lever. The lever is the *structure and individualized guidance* that make practice "deliberate" (E046), and the honest ceiling on what any tutor can promise is lower than marketing implies.

5. **"AI can grade your playing" / grade everything you can measure.**
   - **Evidence:**
     - Human experts themselves agree only moderately, with halo effects and severity differences (E016–E021).
     - Machines agree with experts on pitch and rhythm (E085, E086) but localise problems poorly (problem-note F1 .705, E086).
     - No stage-differentiated criteria exist (E022).
   - **Candidate:** grade and adapt only on dimensions with demonstrated reliability against a multi-rater panel (timing, pitch, chord-change accuracy). Present expressive dimensions as *prompts for self-review*, not scores.

6. **"Adaptive/personalized beats one-size-fits-all."**
   - **For:** ITS beat conventional classes and textbooks (E001–E003); mastery gating ES 0.52 (E005).
   - **Against:**
     - ITS are not better than individual human tutoring (E004).
     - Matching to learner type d = 0.04 (E009–E011).
     - The only music adaptive-vs-fixed test was null, n=19 (E007).
     - Effects inflate on locally made tests (E002).
   - **Candidate:** for this learner the right comparator is a good fixed curriculum plus a weekly teacher, not "no instruction". Adaptivity should be limited to mastery gating and step-level feedback on checkable skills; fine-grained adaptive sequencing is unproven.

7. **"The tutor can diagnose your bottleneck."**
   - **Evidence:**
     - Individual learning-rate estimates are unreliable without deep per-skill data (E014, E015).
     - A narrow interval tutor needed hundreds of items to confirm mastery (E008).
     - No study measures teacher agreement on bottlenecks (gap).
   - **Candidate:** treat diagnosis as a *testable hypothesis*. The tutor proposes a bottleneck, runs a short targeted probe, and keeps or discards it. It should not claim a measured diagnosis.

8. **"Interleave your practice" (desirable difficulties applied wholesale to music).**
   - **For:** non-music evidence (E061–E063); interval novices without aids (E012); clarinet (E057).
   - **Against:** null results in proficient pianists (E058), with aids (E012 Exp 1) and at retention (E060).
   - **Candidate:** interleave discrimination-heavy material (chord qualities, intervals, similar voicings). Do not assume a benefit for refining motor passages at intermediate level. Since learners misjudge schedules (E013), the tutor should schedule rather than ask.
