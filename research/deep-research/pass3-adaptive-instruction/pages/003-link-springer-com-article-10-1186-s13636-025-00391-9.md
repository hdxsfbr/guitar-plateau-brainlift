Abstract
--------

### Background

Music training for learners has improved greatly in recent years with the inclusion of information technology and optimization methods. The improvements focus on assisted learning, instruction suggestions, and performance assessments.

### Purpose

An adaptive instructive suggestion method (AISM) using deep neural fuzzy control (FC) is introduced in this paper to provide persistent assistance for technology-based music classrooms. This proposed method reduces learning errors by pursuing instructions based on the learner’s level. The instructions are adaptable depending on the error and level independent of different suggestions. The suggestions are replicated for similar issues across various music learning classrooms, retaining the constant fuzzification.

### Materials and methods

The fuzzy control deviates at every new level, and errors are identified over the deviations from the instructions pursued. This control process verifies the input based on instruction deviations to prevent error repetitions. Therefore, the fuzzification relies on error normalization using common adaptive suggestions for different learning sessions. If the fuzzy control fails to match the existing instruction pursued, then new instructions are augmented to reduce errors that serve as the FC constraint. This constraint is pursued by unresolved previous errors to improve learning efficacy.

### Results

Thus, compared to other methods, the system improves adaptability by 13.9%, efficiency analysis by 9.02%, and constraint detection by 10.26%.

### 3.3 Adaptive instructive suggestion method (AISM) using fuzzy control (FC)

This AIS method aims to improve the learning efficacy of music training with fuzzy logic control based on improved assisted learning, instruction suggestions, and performance assessment concerning time. The proposed method provides persistent assistance for information technology-based music classrooms through FC. The method aims to reduce learning errors by processing a dynamic approach for adaptive music instruction. The AISM using FC is a dynamic approach that leverages fuzzy logic control principles to provide personalized and adaptive guidance in various domains. AISM begins by collecting relevant user or environment data, typically through sensors or input devices. Based on fuzzy sets and rules, fuzzy control is employed to process this data and generate context-aware recommendations or instructions. Fuzzy logic allows for handling imprecise or uncertain information, making it suitable for scenarios with varying conditions. The system then adapts these suggestions in real time based on user feedback or changing circumstances, ensuring that the guidance remains relevant and effective. Overall, AISM with FC provides a flexible and responsive way to offer tailored guidance and support across diverse applications, from robotics and automation to personalized education and decision-making systems. 6 Conclusion
------------

This article discussed the implication of the adaptive instructive suggestion method for improving the adaptability of music instructions. The proposed method integrates musical instructions across various sessions using fuzzy control optimization. The adaptability feature is precisely defined by accounting for the fewer errors it generates during the training session. The error computation is performed using fuzzy control optimization by identifying various learning and teaching factors associated with the musical classroom. The errors are suppressed using constraint reducing using multiple deviations across repetitions. First, the deviation to error and error to repetition factors are computed. The computations are fuzzified using control optimization to identify its instruction assimilation and failures. Based on the remaining constraints, the unresolved instructions for the errors are replaced with new suggestions. Such suggestions are adaptable for various new sessions, preventing variations and for multiple existing suggestions. The similar methods in the comparative analysis show that the proposed method improves efficacy analysis, instruction adaptability, and constraint detection by 9.02%, 13.9%, and 10.26%, respectively. The error and analysis time are reduced by 16.63% and 10.6%, respectively.

The proposed method faces limitations related to data, privacy, complexity, potential biases, resource requirements, resistance to change, cultural considerations, and the need for a thorough evaluation. Addressing these limitations is critical to realizing its full potential in music education.

To address the limitations that have been identified, the study should improve the methods used to collect diverse musical performance data, implement strong measures to protect privacy, simplify the complexity of the system, detect and minimize biases through algorithms, optimize the use of resources, encourage innovation to overcome resistance to change, consider cultural sensitivity, and conduct comprehensive evaluations. These tactics are designed to promote the system’s efficiency, user satisfaction, and influence in adaptive music training. They also aim to ensure transparency, fairness, and ongoing improvement in future research efforts.

For future work, researchers could focus on enhancing the system’s data acquisition methods, ensuring the collection of high-quality and diverse musical performance data to improve the accuracy of recommendations. Additionally, refining the privacy and security measures to address concerns related to data handling and student privacy is crucial. Finally, exploring ways to integrate real-time feedback mechanisms, such as audio analysis and live interaction, could enhance the system’s effectiveness in music instruction, providing students with immediate guidance and assessment during their learning process.
