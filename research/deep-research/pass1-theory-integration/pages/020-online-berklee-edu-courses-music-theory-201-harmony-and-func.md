## Course Description

Building on the foundational concepts introduced in _Music Theory 101_, this course is designed to expand your understanding of music theory at the intermediate level. You’ll explore the rhythmic, harmonic, and melodic elements that form the backbone of musical styles such as jazz, pop, blues, and rock. Whether you’re a performer, composer, or simply passionate about music, this course will provide you with the tools to elevate your skills and deepen your musical insight. Through a combination of ear training exercises, musical examples, and guided activities, you’ll enhance your ability to listen, read, analyze, and write music with greater precision.

[Read More](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/music-theory-201-harmony-and-function#)

This course goes beyond theoretical knowledge, providing practical exercises and personalized feedback from your instructor to help you integrate these skills into your musical practice. By mastering these concepts, you’ll be better equipped to create and interpret music with confidence and creativity.

**By the end of this course, you will be able to:**

- Read and write rhythms that include anticipations and articulations
- Write and analyze progressions in harmonic minor
- Compose a pentatonic melody over a basic blues progression
- Analyze simple song forms, including binary and blues forms

## Syllabus

_Lesson 1 The Imaginary Bar Line_

- The Imaginary Bar Line
- Note Values Smaller than the Half Note
- Rest Values and the Imaginary Bar Line

_Lesson 2 Diatonic Triads and Seventh Chords in Major_

- The Construction of Diatonic Triads
- Diatonic Seventh Chords

_Lesson 3 The Harmonic Function of Diatonic Chords_

- The Terms Tonic, Subdominant, and Dominant
- Harmonic Cadences
- The IImin7 V7 IMaj7 Progression

_Lesson 4 Additional Chord Types_

- Two Additional Dominant 7 Chords
- Two Additional Major Chords
- Two Additional Minor Chords

_Lesson 5 Harmonic Function and Voice Leading of Additional Chord Types_

- The Major and Minor 6th Chords
- The Minor and Augmented Major 7th Chords
- The Augmented 7th and Dominant 7(sus4)

_Lesson 6 Diatonic Chords in Harmonic Minor_

- Diatonic Chords in Harmonic Minor
- Diatonic Function in Harmonic Minor
- The II V7 I Progression in Minor

_Lesson 7 The Upper Structures of Harmony/Tensions_

- The Theory of Tensions
- Altered Tensions
- The Melodic Use of Tensions

_Lesson 8 The Harmonic Use of Tensions_

- Substituting the 9th for the Root
- Substituting the 13th for the 5th
- Using Altered Tensions
- Using the 11th

_Lesson 9 The Rhythmic Anticipation and Articulations_

- The Rhythmic Anticipation
- Short Durational Value Anticipations
- Articulation Markings

_Lesson 10 Simple Binary Song Form_

- Examples of Simple Binary Form
- Other Types of Variation and Contrast

_Lesson 11 The Blues Form and Style_

- The Blues Form
- The Blues Style and Its Effect on Harmony
- The Blues Style and Its Effect on Melody

_Lesson 12 A Closer Look at the Dominant Seventh Chord_

- Voice Leading Dominant Seventh Chords with Tensions
- Stripping the Dominant Seventh Chord down to the Basics
- Adding a Third Voice to the Dominant Seventh Chord

![Paul Schmeling](https://online.berklee.edu/user/avatar?user_id=13934)

[Paul Schmeling](https://online.berklee.edu/faculty/paul-schmeling)

Author

Paul Schmeling (1938-2024) was a master pianist, interpreter, improviser and arranger who inspired countless students since he began teaching at Berklee in 1961. He has performed or recorded with jazz greats such as Clark Terry, Rebecca Parris, George Coleman, Carol Sloane, Frank Foster, Art Farmer, Herb Pomeroy, Phil Wilson, Dick Johnson and Slide Hampton. In the 1990s, the Paul Schmeling Trio released two inventive and critically acclaimed albums, interpreting the music of Hoagy Carmichael and songs associated with Frank Sinatra. He was chair of the piano department for 40 years, and he co-authored the [_Berklee Practice Method: Keyboard_(2001)](http://www.berkleemusic.com/store/product?product_id=11343) and [_Instant Keyboard_(2002)](http://www.berkleemusic.com/store/product?product_id=11517) and on his own he wrote [_Berklee Music Theory: Book 1_](http://www.berkleemusic.com/store/product?product_id=2780849).
