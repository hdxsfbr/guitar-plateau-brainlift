### Working purpose (revise as evidence develops)

Determine how an AI-supported tutor should integrate and sequence technique, music theory, ear training, repertoire, and improvisation for adult intermediate guitarists with a two-hour daily practice budget over two years. Use research to decide how instruction should adapt to individual needs and where review of recorded exercises can provide trustworthy, useful feedback. Measure lasting improvement and application to unfamiliar music, rather than only success inside the tutor.

## 2. Decisions explicitly made with Andre

Genre, exact target proficiency, teacher access, equipment, and intended tutor budget remain open. AI disagreement is not evidence that a claim is correct. Do not manufacture disagreement or a supposed consensus. The assignment requires two POVs; exploring more candidates first is fine.

## 5. What Elicit found, and what it did not establish

Treat this section as a summary of the supplied AI outputs, not independently verified scientific conclusions.

- Direct controlled evidence on plateaued adult intermediate guitarists and durable improvisation transfer was sparse in Elicit's retrieved set. This is a search result, not proof of absence across the literature.
- Phrase imitation/variation and integrated listening/singing/playing were plausible design choices. Superiority to scales was not established, and is no longer the question Andre wants to center.
- Blocked versus variable/interleaved music practice had mixed, task-dependent results. Some piano/clarinet results favored variation on selected outcomes; a percussion study reported equivalent later retention/transfer.
- Video-supported self-review changed classical guitarists' self-evaluative comments. That is not the same as demonstrated improvement in improvisation, retention, or transfer.
- A music self-regulation review was reported to support planning, monitoring, strategy use, and reflection. Check its original methods and results before making causal or population-specific claims.
- The two-year research prompt generated additional source leads and a citation audit, but **no substantive two-year curriculum/research synthesis has been delivered**.

The audit reports 16 claims: 11 supported, 4 partially supported, 1 citation mismatch, with 14 assessed at abstract level. These are Elicit's judgments. Check originals for claims that determine the final POVs.

## 6. Priority research questions

1. What distinguishes intermediate, advanced, and expert guitarists across hearing, technique, rhythm, harmony, expression, repertoire, and interaction?
2. How do these skills develop together? Which prerequisites or instructional sequences are supported by evidence?
3. How should scales/arpeggios, technical exercises, theory, ear training, transcription, repertoire, composition, improvisation, and playing with others be integrated?
4. What evidence favors a fixed curriculum, individualized adaptation, or a combination? Can learner bottlenecks actually be diagnosed reliably?
5. How do feedback timing, amount, modality, and learner control interact with expertise and task?
6. Which roles can automated feedback serve, and which require teachers or other musicians?
7. What supports adherence and sustainable practice over two years? Evaluate workload and injury-prevention evidence appropriately.
8. What should progress at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months look like? Treat proposed milestones as design choices until validated.
9. What evidence would show independent skill, delayed retention, and transfer rather than memorization or dependence on prompts?
10. What findings challenge documented teaching/product advice strongly enough to support two useful spiky POVs?

Preserve openness to outcomes beyond improvisation. Distinguish musical competence from theory quiz accuracy and from automatic note-detection accuracy.

## 7. Recording assessment: desired scope and unresolved feasibility

Investigate this capability; no assessment system has been implemented or validated in this conversation.

| Task | Candidate assessment targets | Key cautions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scales/arpeggios | Expected notes, omissions, timing, execution at a target tempo | Transcription errors, articulation, string noise; audio alone may not reveal fingering |
| Rhythm exercises | Intended rhythm, tempo stability, timing deviations | Intended swing/groove versus literal grid alignment; capture latency |
| Phrase imitation | Pitch, rhythm, selected articulation | Define which differences count as errors versus interpretation |
| Bends/sustains | Target pitch, pitch stability, vibrato characteristics | Intentional expressive variation and pitch-tracker reliability |
| Constrained improvisation | Motif development, target tones, exercise constraints | A non-chord tone is not automatically a musical error |
| Open improvisation | Descriptive phrasing/expression feedback | Rubric validity, style, rater disagreement, and uncertainty |

Proposed feedback interaction: timestamp → observation → replay → correction cue → retry. Prefer understandable per-dimension feedback to an unexplained global score. These are design proposals, not research findings.

Validate separately:

- **Measurement accuracy:** can the system reliably recover the needed information from actual recordings?
- **Assessment validity:** do scores/observations agree sufficiently with appropriately qualified raters and explicit task criteria?
- **Learning benefit:** does using the feedback improve later independent performance and transfer?

Test across clean/distorted guitar, chords/single notes, recording quality, instrument/style differences, and backing-track bleed. Withhold or qualify a grade when the evidence is unreliable. Recorded/asynchronous review is sufficient for the current scope; real-time feedback is not required.

## 8. Candidate spiky POVs (not selected or proven)

Two directions were proposed most recently:

1. **Theory mastery should require musical application.** Advancement should require hearing and playing a concept in an unfamiliar context, not only naming it or answering a quiz. Need to establish which real practices this challenges; otherwise it is sensible mainstream advice rather than a spiky POV.
2. **An AI tutor should earn the right to grade each skill separately.** Score only dimensions with validated assessments; use descriptive feedback elsewhere. This may be sound engineering rather than a distinctive educational disagreement. Investigate whether it changes an actual product or pedagogical decision and whether evidence supports it.

Another candidate is adapting practice to the learner's current limiting skill instead of keeping fixed time allocations. This depends on diagnostic reliability and could already be mainstream.

Earlier scales-versus-phrases and reduced-feedback ideas must not anchor the search. Andre explicitly corrected the scale-replacement framing. Keep candidate POVs disposable. Seek documented disagreement, evidence, practical consequences, strongest counterevidence, and falsifiable predictions.

## 9. Source leads to prioritize

Full bibliographic records are in the CSV. These are leads, not endorsements of study quality:

- **Solstad, Expertise in Jazz Guitar Improvisation (2020):** direct guitar/expert context; practitioner and qualitative evidence. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780429022692
- **Instrumental Jazz Improvisation Development (2016):** reported relationships among aural imitation, technical facility, theory, and improvisation; inspect design/confounding. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022429416664897
- **The Effects of Two Jazz Pedagogical Approaches… (2022):** practice-based versus theory-based instruction in high-school musicians; do not conflate this with removing theory. https://doi.org/10.2979/jazzeducrese.1.1.05
- **Marino & Chinn (2023):** instructional sequencing, cooperative combos, feedback, and reported transfer in younger beginning improvisers. https://doi.org/10.1177/00224294221150019
- **Boucher, Creech & Dubé (2019):** classical guitar video feedback and self-evaluation. https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735619842374
- **Carter & Grahn (2016):** blocked/interleaved practice in advanced clarinetists. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251
- **Wopereis et al. (2015), The development of improvisational expertise in jazz musicians:** qualitative developmental trajectories; see CSV URL.
- **Deliberate-practice reviews/debate:** inspect definitions, retrospective hour estimates, confounding, and selection; explained variance is not a percentage of an individual's ability caused by practice.
- **From Practice to Reflection (2025):** verify the review's methods and scope. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence13120162
- **Development of a rubric for collegiate jazz improvisation performance assessment (2016):** see CSV; potential measurement lead.
- **Adaptive Sequencing in Interval Ear Training: A Multi-Armed Bandit Approach (2026):** see CSV; verify publication and evidence before use. https://doi.org/10.5220/0014726800004021

Additional web leads found after Elicit's run:

- **Ground truths: challenges and opportunities in developing an AI guitar assistant (2025):** primary research relevant to assessment and stakeholder needs. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1549335/full
- **Play it again with feeling: computer feedback in musical communication of emotions:** study involving semi-professional jazz/rock guitarists; retrieve original methods/results. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16802890/
- **Spotify Basic Pitch:** audio-to-MIDI/pitch-bend extraction component, not a validated grading system. https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch

## 10. Research tooling decisions and limitations

- Candidate recursive web-research implementation: https://github.com/dzhng/deep-research
  - Its `src/deep-research.ts` was inspected during this conversation. It recursively generates queries, extracts learnings, follows questions, and writes a report.
  - In the inspected version, learnings are strings and visited URLs are collected separately and appended to the report. Claim-to-source relationships are not explicitly preserved in the data model.
  - If used, preserve a structured evidence record at extraction time. A final bibliography does not establish support for every claim.
  - Requires model/search infrastructure and potentially paid API usage; open-source code does not mean free execution. No setup, keys, purchases, or research runs with this repo have been performed here.
- PaperQA: https://github.com/Future-House/paper-qa . Candidate for querying a curated full-text corpus with citations, not an automatic replacement for source discovery or critical appraisal.
- Co-STORM/STORM: https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm . Candidate for exploring perspectives and knowledge organization; no local test performed.
- Elicit: useful discovery output obtained, but user reports quota exhausted. Do not claim superiority over the dzhng repo: no head-to-head experiment was run.
- LangChain Open Deep Research was observed as archived August 21, 2026. Recheck before any tooling decision based on maintenance state.

Choose the smallest workflow that completes the research. Ordinary browsing plus disciplined evidence files may suffice. Do not spend the assignment building a research platform.
